AQUATIC EXPOSURE / CONCEPTUAL ILLUSTRATION
The central finding

Kumanox 19’s ozone reaction products were less toxic than 6PPD’s in some comparisons. They still caused acute mortality in rainbow trout.

01 / The experiment

Two samples.
Three test species.

The laboratory tested Kumanox 19 and a manufacturer-supplied ozonated sample using short-term survival bioassays. Results were compared with 6PPD testing reported in 2025. This was a chemical-sample study; it did not test runoff from finished tires.

02 / Results

Ozonation changes
the toxicity pattern.

Untreated Kumanox 19 and untreated 6PPD showed broadly similar toxicity across the three species. For Kumanox 19, ozonation reduced toxicity to the invertebrates but increased toxicity to rainbow trout. Lower LC50 values mean greater acute toxicity.

The report describes Kumanox 19 ozonate as less toxic than 6PPD ozonate to trout and amphipods. The water-flea comparison is inconclusive because different concentration ranges were tested. These are comparisons between studies, not a single simultaneous experiment.

03 / Reading the evidence

Comparative improvement is one piece of the assessment.

These results identify acute hazards under laboratory conditions. They do not establish environmental safety or predict risk at a particular stormwater outfall.

Interpretation: environmental risk also depends on exposure, chemical fate, species sensitivity, and effects beyond short-term mortality.

Nominal concentrations, not measured exposure

The concentrations were based on preparation amounts. The report does not confirm actual chemical concentrations throughout exposure.

Ozonation method was not supplied

Kumho Petrochemical supplied both samples. The laboratory received no information about the ozonation method. “Ozonate” refers to the supplied ozone-reacted sample; it should not be equated with an isolated, identified quinone.

Short-term survival, three species

The study evaluated acute mortality. It did not assess chronic exposure, reproduction, growth, or coho salmon, and it did not test finished-tire runoff.

Laboratory deviations and uncertainty

The report records deviations involving trout exposure volume, temperature, dissolved oxygen, and a fish lost during renewal. Controls met survival criteria. The methods, detailed results, and appendices should be consulted when assessing these findings.

04 / Questions to carry forward

What would make the next assessment stronger?

These are research questions prompted by the study and its limitations, rather than conclusions demonstrated by the experiment.

How do Kumanox 19 and its transformation products affect coho and other sensitive aquatic species?

Which compounds are present after ozonation, and at what measured concentrations?

What leaches from finished tires under realistic wear, weathering, and runoff conditions?

Do repeated or long-term exposures affect survival, growth, or reproduction?
